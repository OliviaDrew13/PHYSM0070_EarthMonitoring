- epistemic uncertainty: incomplete knowledge, model limitations, data scarcity
- probabilistic methods: Bayesian inference, mixture density networks ect. probabilistic relationships/distributions
- simulation-based techniques: model ensembles, sensitivity analysis. variability across model config, data samples, parameter settings

Evaluation
- depends on learning paradigm and task type
- Clustering
	- Silhouette Score, Davies–Bouldin Index, Dunn Index, and Adjusted Rand Index (ARI)
- Anomaly detection
	- precision, recall, and F1-score
	- Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (AUC-PR) and the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC-ROC) 
- Classification
	- accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrices
- Regression
	- Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)

Data Limitations
- geographic/ecosystem bias: publicly available datasets are geographically concentrated in certain area
- anthropogenic bias: historical datasets overrepresent human developed areas and underrepresent remote areas. may associate fire risk with urban areas and underrepresent risk in ecologically prone areas
- temporal resolution and alignment issues: different temporal resolutions and inconsistent update cycles pose major challenges
	- Z. Xu, J. Li, S. Cheng, X. Rui, Y. Zhao, H. He, L. Xu Wildfire risk prediction: A review
	- S. Hauser, L. Augner, A. Schmitt Perfect labelling: A review and outlook of label optimization techniques in dynamic earth observation

Label quality and completeness
- satellite-based datasets: rely on thermal anomaly detection, false +ves and -ves
- small scale fires are frequently emitted from global datasets
- label noise introduces significant uncertainty into model training and evaluation
- can skew risk assessments
- papers to checkout
	- S. Yang, Q. Huang, M. Yu Advancements in remote sensing for active fire detection: a review of datasets and methods
	- D. Van Wees, G.R. van Der Werf, J.T. Randerson, N. Andela, Y. Chen, D.C. Morton The role of fire in global forest loss dynamics

Lack of standardised benchmarks
- no universally adopted benchmarks
- many datasets lack ground-truth verification or confidence scores
- absence of human-verified fire reports or structured incident data (e.g., from forestry services or fire departments) reduces the reliability of long-term wildfire modelling efforts
